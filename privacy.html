2. Overview

Blockbeat 808 is a game without an account of its own. We run no servers of our own and receive no data about you. Your saved game, high scores and settings (sound, vibration, design) are stored on your device. You can delete them at any time by clearing the app's data or uninstalling it.

Personal data is processed in only two places, in both cases by Google as described below: for ads via Google AdMob (section 3) and, if you use Google Play Games, for the online leaderboard and the cloud backup of your progress (section 4). We use no other analytics or tracking services.

3. Advertising with Google AdMob

Provider: Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland (“Google”).

The app shows a banner and, if you choose, a video ad that lets you continue a lost round once. For this, Google processes in particular:

  • your device's advertising ID (Android: Advertising ID; iPhone: the IDFA, only if you allow tracking when Apple asks, see section 4),
  • your IP address and the approximate location derived from it,
  • device information such as model, operating system, language and app version,
  • information about which ads were shown and tapped.

Purposes: serving and measuring ads, frequency capping, protection against fraud and abuse and – only with your consent – personalised advertising.

Legal bases: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR and Section 25(1) TDDDG for accessing information on your device). Without consent, Google shows only limited, non-personalised ads where permitted; processing necessary for this, e.g. fraud prevention, is based on legitimate interests (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Google may transfer data to the USA. Google is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework; the EU Commission's standard contractual clauses also apply.

More: Google Privacy Policy and How Google uses data for advertising.

4. Google Play Games (leaderboard and cloud backup)

Provider: Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland (“Google”).

On Android devices with Google Play Games, the app signs you in automatically with your Play Games profile. The game also works without it. While you are signed in, the following is processed via Google:

  • your Play Games profile (gamer name, avatar, player ID),
  • your scores for the online leaderboard, together with your device's country (from your phone's region setting, not your location), so that a flag appears next to your name,
  • if you agree, your Play Games friends list (gamer names only), for the “Friends” leaderboard,
  • a cloud backup of your progress (diamonds, unlocks, choices, high scores, statistics, tasks) in “Saved Games” of your Google account, so that it comes back after a new phone or reinstall,
  • analytics and diagnostics data that Google collects for the stability of the service.

Who sees your name and scores on the leaderboard is up to you in the Google Play Games profile settings (“Everyone”, “Friends only” or “Only you”). We do not receive this data on servers of our own; it stays with Google.

Legal bases: providing the game features you use (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR) and our legitimate interest in a leaderboard and backup (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR); for the friends list, your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR). Google may transfer data to the USA (EU-US Data Privacy Framework, standard contractual clauses).

Deleting and signing out: in your Play Games profile or your Google Account, including the data of this game only. More: Google Privacy Policy.

5. Consent and withdrawal

On first launch in the EU, EEA, Switzerland and the UK, the app asks for your consent with Google's consent dialog (User Messaging Platform). You can change or withdraw your choice at any time in the app's start menu under “Privacy settings”. Withdrawal applies to the future.

On iPhone, Apple's system also asks whether the app may track you (App Tracking Transparency). Only if you allow it does Google receive your device's IDFA. You can change this at any time in the iPhone's Settings under Privacy & Security → Tracking. Without permission Google cannot use the IDFA; to measure ad success, Apple's SKAdNetwork may be used, which reports results without identifying you.
